# 1. The Birth of a State (1996)

On **1 October 1996**, a new name was added to the map of Nigeria. Ekiti State was created out of Ondo State, with Ado-Ekiti as its capital.<sup>\[1\]\[58\]</sup> The state government says it was created alongside five other states and began with 16 Local Government Areas.**‡**<sup>\[58\]</sup> For the towns of Ekiti, it was the start of a state of their own.

Statehood arrived under military rule. **Lt. Col. Mohammed Bawa** was the first administrator, serving from 7 October 1996 to August 1998.<sup>\[1\]</sup> **Navy Captain Atanda Yusuf** followed and served until civilian rule began on 29 May 1999.<sup>\[1\]</sup>

Yusuf is credited with building the complex that houses the Ekiti State House of Assembly, which was inaugurated on 1 June 1999, and with helping bring peace to Ikere-Ekiti after communal strife.**†**<sup>\[4\]</sup>

> **Deep roots**
>
> Ekiti's identity is older than the state. According to tradition, the Udiroko festival of Ado-Ekiti began about 1310 AD.**◊**<sup>\[31\]</sup>

## Democracy returns (1999–2003)

On 29 May 1999 **Otunba Niyi Adebayo** of the Alliance for Democracy became Ekiti's first elected civilian governor, serving until 29 May 2003.<sup>\[1\]</sup>

## Fayose, the first act, and the emergency (2003–2007)

**Ayo Fayose** of the PDP took office on 29 May 2003 after defeating Adebayo.<sup>\[1\]\[7\]</sup> In October 2006 the House of Assembly impeached him. The state's official list records that the Federal Government declared his brief replacement by the Speaker illegal, and sources disagree on whether the impeachment came on 15 or 16 October.**†**<sup>\[1\]\[7\]</sup> **Tunji Olurin** served as administrator from 19 October 2006 to 27 April 2007, appointed after a state of emergency was declared.<sup>\[1\]</sup> Other accounts add that President Obasanjo declared the emergency, suspended the governor, deputy governor and Assembly, and that the National Assembly ratified it on 26 October; these details are not yet confirmed.**†**<sup>\[6\]</sup> **Tope Ademiluyi** was acting governor from 27 April to 29 May 2007.<sup>\[1\]</sup>

## A contested mandate (2007–2010)

**Segun Oni** of the PDP was sworn in on 29 May 2007, and Kayode Fayemi challenged the result at the election tribunal.<sup>\[1\]\[7\]</sup> On 17 February 2009 the Court of Appeal nullified the result in part and ordered reruns in 10 of the 16 local government areas; Speaker **Tunji Odeyemi** served as acting governor until 6 May 2009, when Oni was sworn in again after the rerun.<sup>\[1\]\[42\]</sup> On 15 October 2010 the Court of Appeal declared Fayemi the duly elected governor, and he was sworn in the next day.<sup>\[43\]\[62\]</sup> Ekiti's courts, not just its ballot boxes, had twice decided who would lead.

## Fayemi, Fayose and Fayemi again (2010–2022)

**Kayode Fayemi**, then of the Action Congress of Nigeria, governed from October 2010 to October 2014.<sup>\[1\]\[7\]</sup> In INEC's declaration for the 21 June 2014 election, **Ayo Fayose** (PDP) was declared elected with 203,090 votes to Fayemi's 120,433.<sup>\[48\]</sup> Opponents argued that his 2006 impeachment made him ineligible. On 14 April 2015 the Supreme Court dismissed the APC's appeal; reports say it held that impeachment is not a ground for disqualification and that the 2006 impeachment panel was illegally constituted, though the judgment itself has not been read.**†**<sup>\[65\]\[66\]\[7\]</sup> Fayose's second term ran from 16 October 2014 to 16 October 2018, when Fayemi, now in the APC, began a second term that ran to October 2022.<sup>\[1\]</sup>

## Oyebanji and a second mandate (2022–2026)

**Biodun Oyebanji** of the APC was sworn in on 16 October 2022.<sup>\[55\]</sup> In INEC's declaration for the 18 June 2022 election he was declared elected with 187,057 votes, ahead of Segun Oni (SDP) with 82,211 and Bisi Kolawole (PDP) with 67,457.<sup>\[71\]</sup> Oni filed a petition challenging the result; its outcome has not been checked.**‡**<sup>\[56\]</sup>

Oyebanji faced voters again on 20 June 2026. At about 3:13 a.m. on Sunday 21 June, INEC's State Returning Officer, Prof. Adenike Oladiji, declared him winner with **319,224 votes**, against 40,543 for Wole Oluyede (PDP) and 12,872 for Dare Bejide (ADC), with 375,777 valid and 6,332 rejected votes, according to Channels TV, Vanguard and Daily Trust.<sup>\[11\]\[72\]\[67\]</sup> The party totals published by ThisDay's live updates add up to the valid-vote figure only if the PDP total is 40,543, so the 40,533 printed by a few outlets is almost certainly a typographical error.<sup>\[73\]</sup> INEC's own declaration document (Form EC8E) has not yet been consulted, so the result is still marked as awaiting a primary record.**†**<sup>\[70\]</sup>

Reported claims and disputes are kept apart from the result. The ADC candidate alleged irregularities, which Oyebanji rejected,**‡**<sup>\[11\]</sup> observers' concerns about vote-buying and intimidation were reported,**‡**<sup>\[36\]</sup> and Yiaga Africa's observation mission raised concerns about the consistency of the materials INEC deployed after late candidate-list changes.**‡**<sup>\[11\]</sup> Premium Times and allAfrica describe him as the first Ekiti governor to secure consecutive re-election, and Dataphyte notes that no incumbent had won a second consecutive term before.**‡**<sup>\[68\]\[12\]\[69\]</sup> Before the vote, Legit.ng and allAfrica noted that no incumbent had won re-election since the 1999 return to elected government, and that Adebayo, the first governor, served a single term.**‡**<sup>\[36\]</sup> These are media characterisations, not INEC statements, and they are not confirmed against INEC or other historical records. As of September 2026, Oyebanji's current term runs to October 2026.

# 3. Building Ekiti: Landmarks and Infrastructure

Several of Ekiti's landmark projects began under one government and were finished under another.

## Ikogosi Warm Springs

At Ikogosi, in Ekiti West, a warm spring and a cold spring meet and keep their different temperatures as they flow together.<sup>\[16\]</sup> According to the resort's own history, its modern story began in 1952, when Southern Baptist missionary Rev. John Sydney McGee visited the source and later secured land for a youth camp. Ondo State acquired the site from the Baptist Mission in 1978, and Ekiti State took it over after the state's creation.**†**<sup>\[15\]\[16\]</sup>

The Fayemi administration began rehabilitation in **July 2011**,**†**<sup>\[14\]</sup> and the refurbished resort opened to the public around October 2012, with full operations announced for April 2013.**†**<sup>\[41\]</sup> Management was later handed to Glocient Hospitality, a Cavista Holdings subsidiary, under a 15-year build-operate-transfer concession; the signing year is disputed between 2022 and 2023.**†**<sup>\[40\]\[37\]</sup>

## The Ekiti Agro-Allied International Cargo Airport

Governor Oyebanji describes the airport in Ado-Ekiti as a shared project of four administrations: Segun Oni conceived the idea, Ayo Fayose acquired and cleared the site, Kayode Fayemi began construction, and his own government completed it.**‡**<sup>\[18\]</sup> Construction began in late 2019.<sup>\[19\]</sup>

In October 2022, shortly before leaving office, Fayemi commissioned the airport in a ceremony, and the state government of the day acknowledged the facility was still under construction.**†**<sup>\[21\]</sup> The Nigerian Civil Aviation Authority granted a six-month approval for commercial operations in early October 2025,<sup>\[19\]</sup> and on **10 December 2025** United Nigeria Airlines operated the first commercial flight, connecting Ado-Ekiti with Abuja and Lagos.<sup>\[18\]\[19\]</sup> The state government says it spent about ₦49.77 billion of its own funds on the project.**‡**<sup>\[18\]</sup>

> **Source conflict: commissioning year**
>
> Daily Trust gives October 2023 for the commissioning ceremony,<sup>\[20\]</sup> but contemporaneous SaharaReporters coverage is dated 22 October 2022<sup>\[21\]</sup> and Fayemi's tenure ended on 16 October 2022. The dataset records October 2022 with the basis documented and keeps the entry flagged as conflicting (EK-035) until an official record settles it.

## Health and teaching hospitals

The Federal Medical Centre in Ido-Ekiti is recorded as established in July 1998 from a general hospital that dated from 1954.**†**<sup>\[46\]</sup> In October 2014 the federal government approved its upgrade to a teaching hospital for the clinical training of Afe Babalola University students,**†**<sup>\[44\]</sup> and a National Assembly Act later listed Ido among new federal teaching hospitals, although the Ido-specific date is unconfirmed.**†**<sup>\[47\]</sup> In the private sector, the 400-bed Afe Babalola University Teaching Hospital in Ado-Ekiti was inaugurated in October 2017, with Vice President Yemi Osinbajo represented by the Health Minister.**†**<sup>\[45\]</sup> In October 2024 Governor Oyebanji flagged off construction of an 80-bed multipurpose complex at the Ekiti State University Teaching Hospital.<sup>\[60\]</sup>

# 4. Festivals of Identity

Festivals are among the ways Ekiti's towns mark their identity and history, and many are far older than the state.

## Udiroko: the new year of Ado-Ekiti

Udiroko is celebrated annually in Ado-Ekiti and gathers the town's sons and daughters at the palace of the Ewi.<sup>\[31\]</sup> According to tradition, it dates to about 1310 AD, during the reign of Oba Awamaro, the first Ewi of modern Ado-Ekiti; the name comes from the great Iroko tree beneath which people converge, and the festival is nicknamed Olorunborun as a day of thanksgiving to God.**◊**<sup>\[31\]</sup> Tradition also holds that it marks the first day of the Ado calendar, with warlords displaying their prowess, gifts of kola nut and walnut shared, and an address from the Ewi that sets the direction for the coming year.**◊**<sup>\[31\]</sup>

Recent editions are documented in the news. In 2024, senators donated ₦110 million toward Ado-Ekiti's development,**†**<sup>\[33\]</sup> and the 2026 edition was celebrated in August.**†**<sup>\[32\]</sup> In November 2025 the Ewi, Oba Rufus Adejugbe Aladesanmi III, marked 35 years on the throne and his 80th birthday with a five-day programme;<sup>\[53\]</sup> the governor's statement credits him with a pivotal role in the campaign for Ekiti's creation, a claim that is not independently verified.**‡**<sup>\[53\]</sup>

## A state of festivals

State cultural listings and news coverage name many other festivals. None is yet in the dataset, so this list is unconfirmed:**†**<sup>\[33\]\[34\]</sup>

- **Olosunta** in Ikere-Ekiti, celebrated annually; Olosunta is also the name of one of Ikere's well-known hills

- **Odun Oba** in Ikere and **Ita Ijero** in Ijero-Ekiti

- **Okorobo** in Ifaki-Ekiti and the **Ajagbo** dance in Isinbode-Ekiti

- **Ladunwo** masquerade in Oke-Imesi and **Iromo** festival in Igede-Ekiti

- **Epa** masquerade in Isan-Ekiti

Each is a candidate for its own entry on the Culture layer, needing a town, a date range and a reliable source.

# 5. Education: The Fountain of Knowledge

Ekiti is popularly nicknamed the "Fountain of Knowledge," though no official source for the slogan has yet been found.**†** Its institutions tell a layered story.

**Ekiti State University (EKSU)** began on 30 March 1982 as Obafemi Awolowo University, Ado-Ekiti, founded by the Ondo State government of Governor Michael Adekunle Ajasin.<sup>\[23\]\[24\]</sup> It later became Ondo State University in 1985, the University of Ado-Ekiti in 1999 and Ekiti State University in 2011; the later names rest on Wikipedia and are not yet confirmed by the university.**†**<sup>\[24\]</sup>

The **Federal University Oye-Ekiti (FUOYE)** was established in 2011, according to the university's own website.<sup>\[57\]</sup> Wikipedia adds campuses at Oye-Ekiti and Ikole-Ekiti and a first graduating set in 2015.**†**<sup>\[25\]</sup> **Afe Babalola University**, a private university in Ado-Ekiti, was established in 2009.<sup>\[63\]</sup>

**Bamidele Olumilua University of Education, Science and Technology (BOUESTI)** in Ikere-Ekiti began as the Ondo State College of Education, established on 5 December 1977.<sup>\[27\]</sup> Governor Fayemi signed into law in 2020 the bill upgrading it to a university, and by January 2021 it was operating under its present name, with Prof. Victor Adeoluwa as first Vice-Chancellor.<sup>\[27\]\[50\]</sup> The exact date of the upgrade is unconfirmed.

A 2022 survey by the Nigerian government and UNICEF (MICS6) is reported to put Ekiti's out-of-school rate at 2 percent, the second lowest in the country after Imo; the survey report itself has not been read.**†**<sup>\[61\]</sup>

# 6. Economy, Care and Innovation

**The Ekiti Knowledge Zone.** Governor Oyebanji states that the technology and innovation hub was conceived on 12 August 2013 under Governor Fayemi.**‡†**<sup>\[38\]</sup> The African Development Bank announced an \$80 million commitment on 22 February 2024,<sup>\[39\]</sup> and the sod was turned on **9 June 2026** at Ago Araromi in Ado-Ekiti by Vice President Kashim Shettima, representing President Tinubu.<sup>\[38\]</sup> The state projects up to 100,000 jobs, a government forecast.**‡**<sup>\[38\]</sup>

**Care for the elderly.** In October 2011 the state began monthly payments of ₦5,000 to indigent citizens aged 65 and over, launched at Ise-Ekiti with about 10,000 beneficiaries.<sup>\[59\]</sup> The state has described the scheme as the first of its kind in Nigeria or Africa; that claim is the government's own.**‡**<sup>\[59\]</sup>

**Agriculture.** In February 2024 the state government announced a partnership with Agbeyewa Farms, a Cavista Holdings subsidiary, starting with a 100-hectare cassava plantation at Ipao-Ekiti.**†**<sup>\[51\]</sup> Later claims of much larger areas come from promoters and legislators and are not verified.**‡**<sup>\[64\]</sup>

**Tourism and sport.** Ikogosi Warm Springs anchors a tourism corridor that also includes the Arinta waterfall at Ipole-Iloro.**†**<sup>\[14\]</sup> In May 2025 Ekiti received the torch of unity for the Ogun 2025 National Sports Festival, and the state said it was building a 900-seat indoor sports complex and renovating Oluyemi Kayode Stadium in Ado-Ekiti.**†**<sup>\[52\]</sup>

# Thirty Years, One Story

Look at Ekiti in its thirtieth year and the threads of this story are visible at once. A House of Assembly complex inaugurated in 1999 marked the start of civilian government.**†**<sup>\[4\]</sup> The Ikogosi resort, under rehabilitation since 2011, has moved into private management.**†**<sup>\[40\]</sup> An airport that began under one governor now receives commercial flights,<sup>\[19\]</sup> and in June 2026 the sod was turned on the Ekiti Knowledge Zone.<sup>\[38\]</sup> According to tradition, Udiroko has been celebrated for some seven centuries,**◊**<sup>\[31\]</sup> and it was marked again in August 2026.**†**<sup>\[32\]</sup> In June 2026 voters returned Governor Oyebanji, which media reports describe as the first consecutive re-election of a governor in the state.**‡**<sup>\[68\]\[12\]</sup>

This document is a draft of that story. It is designed to be extended, event by event, into the verified timeline that powers the Ekiti@30 platform and the Ask Ekiti assistant.

## Why the Google thumbnail links (12 of them) are a hard no
Every `encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com` link is Google's own cached thumbnail, not the source image. They:
- carry no photographer, source page, date, or license — the single most important thing your submission format requires
- are low-resolution crops unfit for print/hero use
- can stop resolving at any time, since they're a cache, not a hosted asset

If a thumbnail is the only lead for a site, treat it as a pointer to go find the real image (reverse image search, or find the original page), not as the image itself.

## Why the Facebook links (4 of them) are rejected too
Each one is a **signed CDN URL** — the `oh=` and `oe=` parameters are an access token and expiry timestamp. These links go dead on their own, usually within hours to days, regardless of copyright. Even setting expiry aside, a photo from someone's Facebook post is owned by that poster, and reposting without asking is exactly what the team's "don't assume online = usable" rule is warning against.

## Why CT-013 and CT-022 are flagged do-not-use rather than just needs-permission
Both trace to art-market infrastructure — HENI (an art platform that deals in auction photography) and what looks like a Sotheby's lot photo. Auction houses and art platforms actively enforce copyright on their photography of high-value pieces; this is a different risk tier from a personal blog or community site, and worth avoiding rather than pursuing permission for.

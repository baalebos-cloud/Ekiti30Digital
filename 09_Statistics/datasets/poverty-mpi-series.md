| Year | Metric | Ekiti figure | National context |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | Per-capita poverty headcount | 62.6% | — |
| 2018/2019 (NLSS) | Poverty headcount / depth / severity | 52.1% / 17.4% / 7.8% | — |
| 2019 (characterization) | Regional ranking | "poorest in the South West" (per NBS-cited commentary, based on IGR and related fiscal indicators) | — |
| 2022 | Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) | 0.125; ~36% of ~1.31M population multidimensionally poor; ranked 5th-lowest nationally | 63% of Nigerians (133M people) multidimensionally poor nationally |

**Reading these together:** The 2010/2018-19 figures use income/per-capita poverty lines and describe a materially poorer state than the 2022 MPI ranking, which uses a broader multidimensional methodology (health, education, living standards) and places Ekiti favourably. Commentary (TheCable) attributes the 2022 result to state governance/programme design outperforming the state's comparatively weak raw fiscal base (35th in FAAC, 32nd in IGR nationally at that time). These are different metrics answering different questions, not a straightforward year-over-year trend — treat any single-number "poverty rate for Ekiti" claim with the methodology attached.

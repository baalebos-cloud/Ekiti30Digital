## Overview
This package covers roughly 30 years of Ekiti State's headline statistics: population/census, poverty and multidimensional poverty, labour force, fiscal/IGR performance, and literacy — the cross-cutting numbers other sectors (Education, Agriculture, Health) already lean on, gathered here with full source trails rather than duplicated inline.

## Key numbers at a glance
- **Population:** 1,647,822 (1991) → 1,750,000 est. (1996, state creation) → 2,384,212 (2006 census, last full count) → 3,270,798 (2016 NPC/NBS projection) → ~3.2M-3.5M (cited variously, 2022-2025, unreconciled).
- **Poverty:** 62.6% per-capita headcount (2010) → 52.1% (2018/19 NLSS) → 2022 Multidimensional Poverty Index of 0.125, ranking Ekiti 5th-best nationally (~36% of population multidimensionally poor).
- **Labour force:** Lowest labour-force participation rate of any state nationally in 2023 (63.4%, vs. national high of 92.3% in Bauchi).
- **Fiscal:** IGR grew 75% in one year (₦17.03bn in 2022 → ₦29.82bn in 2023) — the fastest IGR growth rate of any Nigerian state that year — from a low national base (35th in FAAC, 32nd in IGR as of 2022).
- **Literacy:** Disputed — 84% (state's own StatCloud portal) vs. 95.79% (national media analysis, ranking Ekiti 3rd nationally), unreconciled.

## Recurring themes
1. **A single-census problem** — nearly everything about Ekiti's population past 2006 is a projection, not a count, and the state's own statistics agency's current 3.2M estimate does not cleanly reconcile with the 2016 NPC/NBS-projected 3.27M figure or the ~3.5M figure used in this repository's Health sector package.
2. **"Poor state, good outcomes" tension** — Ekiti repeatedly ranks weak on raw fiscal capacity (IGR, FAAC) yet strong on outcome-based measures (2022 MPI ranking; the maternal-mortality gains documented in 07_Health) — a genuine and sourced pattern, not a data error, worth carrying into any cross-sector "Ekiti performance" narrative.
3. **Fast fiscal improvement, from behind** — the 2023 IGR growth story is real and dramatic (75%, fastest nationally) but should always be stated with its low starting base, or it reads more impressive than the absolute figures support.
4. **A literacy figure genuinely in dispute** — this package deliberately does not pick a winner between the state's 84% and the national media's 95.79%; flagged for owner/reviewer resolution.
5. **Low labour-force participation is Ekiti's most distinctive, least-flattering national ranking** — worth foregrounding in any economic-development narrative, since it is a more unusual finding than the poverty/IGR figures and was not surfaced in prior sector packages.

## What this snapshot does NOT cover (see Gaps for the full list)
Full 16-LGA population/poverty breakdown tables; a formally reconciled current population figure; GDP/GSP (gross state product) time series; health and education-specific indices beyond what is cross-referenced from the Health and Education sector packages; migration statistics; vital registration (births/deaths) completeness rates; the 2023 general-election voter-registration/turnout statistics.

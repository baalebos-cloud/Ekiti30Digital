# Cocoa Revival in Ekiti State, 1970s–2026

Ekiti is currently classified as a **medium cocoa-producing state** in Nigeria
(alongside Edo, Ogun, Oyo, Abia, Delta, Akwa Ibom), behind the high-producing tier
of Ondo, Cross River, and Osun — a national-level classification, not
Ekiti-specific self-reporting (source: peer-reviewed agricultural economics
literature, tier B).

## The arc
- **1970s:** State government sources describe this as Ekiti's cocoa "golden era,"
  when the area was globally reputed as a leading cocoa-producing centre. This
  characterisation comes from current officials looking back, not from
  contemporaneous 1970s data — flagged ATTRIBUTED, not VERIFIED (EK-AGR-004).
- **Post oil-boom decline:** Nationally, cocoa and other cash-crop farming lost
  priority to oil exploration; Nigeria lost international cocoa market share to
  Indonesia and Côte d'Ivoire. In 1999 the Federal Government launched the Cocoa
  Resuscitation Programme (CRP) nationally, providing inputs, training, and
  seedlings through Cocoa Development/Tree Crop Units in producing states,
  including Ekiti.
- **2017 (Fayemi administration):** State government announces a 70-million
  seedling target over 10 years via new Cocoa Clonal Gardens, alongside a N40
  million allocation for clonal garden establishment/rehabilitation (EK-AGR-005).
  No later source in this pass reports on the outcome or completion status of this
  specific 70-million target — treat it as a distinct, unresolved announcement, not
  a programme with a documented endpoint.
- **2024/2025 (Oyebanji administration):** A 50% subsidy scheme on cocoa, cashew,
  and oil-palm seedlings launches, explicitly invoking the 1970s "golden era"
  framing as the goal to restore (EK-AGR-013).
- **2026 (Oyebanji administration):** A new, larger target — 10 million seedlings
  over 10 years (1 million/year) — launches via a USDA TRACE / CRIN / IITA
  partnership, with an explicit traceability, environmental-protection, and
  ethical-production (no deforestation, no child labour) framing aligned to
  international cocoa-export standards. Pilot phase: 28 nursery farmers supported
  with irrigation pumps; hybrid seedling field distribution began mid-2026
  (EK-AGR-014).

## Open question
The 2017 "70 million" and 2026 "10 million" targets are not reconciled in any
source found this pass — they may represent a lapsed programme replaced by a new
one, or inconsistent public messaging across administrations. Do not present them
as a single continuous target without further research (see Gaps).
